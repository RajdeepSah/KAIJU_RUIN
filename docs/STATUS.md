**Phase: vertical slice + multiplayer + combat-art library + onboarding front end + distance/spacing + the kaiju-scaled moveset/guard + the damage rebalance + selective slow motion all in-repo — compiles clean (37 scripts), still needs the on-device playtest.** Session 12 built the owner's **selective slow-motion system** (**D-026**): slow motion now plays on **certain attacks only** — never on an ordinary hit — with an envelope tuned so the transitions read as a camera move rather than a hitch. Two new scripts split the brief's two independent halves: **`TimeDirector.cs`** is *how a shot looks* and **`Cinematics.cs`** is *which hits earn one*. **`Time.timeScale` now has exactly one owner** — every deadline in this fight is an absolute `Time.time` stamp and every movement integrates `Time.deltaTime`, so a single write dilates recovery, stun, walk, the legacy rig clip, the `ProcAnim` envelope and the VFX fades **in step**, which no hand-rolled layer across 37 scripts could guarantee; `GameManager.TogglePause` routes through `TimeDirector.SetPaused` (pause wins and cancels any shot), and hit-stop keeps its own freeze-window pattern and **composes** — a shot's lead phase waits for `CombatFx` to unfreeze, so the beat is impact → freeze → time pours out → hold → return, as one event. **Four things make it not look fake,** and one of them was a correction found by modelling rather than by design: ramps run on **unscaled** time (easing out on scaled time makes the fight wade back to speed); interpolation is **logarithmic**, because the eye tracks the frame-to-frame *ratio* of the scale, not its difference; the envelope is asymmetric in **duration only** (~0.15 s in, 2.5–3.5× longer out) with **smootherstep** on both ramps, whose zero end-derivatives mean a ramp meeting a flat stretch has no kink; and the shot starts **after** the hit-stop bite. The correction: the first attempt front-loaded the entry with an ease-out cubic on the theory that impact should feel abrupt, and modelling the exact per-frame state machine at the 60 fps cap showed it put a **0.17 scale jump in the very first frame** of a 5-frame ramp — a cut wearing a curve's name, i.e. precisely the jarring the directive named. Re-verified after the fix: **first-frame delta 0.010–0.016, last-frame ≤0.0002, every phase boundary continuous to ±0.0002.** **Audio is part of the illusion, not a garnish** — SFX pitch drags almost fully with the scale (`AudioSource.pitch` reaches `PlayOneShot` voices already in flight, so the impact that *caused* the shot stretches with it), music dips slightly and ducks, and **VO not at all**, since the announcer's "K.O." fires into the deepest shot in the game. **"Certain attacks" is a budget problem, and that is where the work is:** rounds now run 42–60 s with 32–45 clean hits and cards fire ~2.7× more often (D-025), so a shot per heavy — or per card — would put the fight in slow motion for a fifth of its length. Hence **at most 2 non-K.O. shots per round, ≥7 s apart** (K.O. exempt), which is **2.4–2.8 s of cinema in a 43–61 s round: 4–6% of its length, 7–9% of its clean hits**, set deliberately at the rare end because too rare is a one-constant fix and too frequent is the failure the brief names. **Every trigger is earned, none rolled** — no RNG anywhere, both because a random crit would make the same read look different on two identical inputs and because it would be the first non-deterministic thing in a sim D-017 wants lockstep-able: **COUNTER** (a *committed* strike landing on someone still inside their own `AttackLockUntil` and **not** already in hitstun — a whiff punish, not the second hit of a combo), **BREAKER** (the hit crossing an opponent below 25% health; self-limiting, since health only falls within a round), **COMEBACK** (a committed strike from under 20% health, once per fighter per round), **SUPER** (a **tier-3 card only** — tiers 1–2 are exactly the ones D-025 made frequent), and **K.O.**, always, deeper and longer when it takes the match. **Damage is untouched**: "critical" classifies the *moment*, not the numbers, because D-025's table is one session old and tuned move-by-move. ### 2026-07-26 — Session 12: selective slow motion for critical hits and finishers (D-026)

- Owner directive: slow motion that plays **only during certain attacks** — critical hits and finishing moves, not every hit — and that looks **smooth and realistic, with natural transitions in and out, so it never feels fake or jarring**. Logged **D-026**.
- **Split the brief into its two independent halves and gave each its own file.** `TimeDirector.cs` = how a shot looks; `Cinematics.cs` = which hits earn one. "Only certain attacks" is a *budget* problem and "never jarring" is an *easing* problem; keeping them apart means the dosage can be re-tuned without touching a curve.
- **`Time.timeScale` became the mechanism, and gained a single owner.** Every deadline in this fight is an absolute `Time.time` stamp and every movement integrates `Time.deltaTime`, so one write dilates recovery, stun, walk, the legacy rig clip, the `ProcAnim` envelope and the VFX fades in step — a hand-rolled dilation layer across 37 scripts would drift the moment one was missed, and drift is what reads as fake. Pause now routes through `TimeDirector.SetPaused` (this reverses `CombatFx`'s stated reason for avoiding timeScale: the answer is single ownership, not avoidance). Hit-stop keeps its freeze-window pattern and **composes** — a shot's lead phase waits for `CombatFx` to unfreeze.
- **Modelling caught the one thing I got wrong.** The first envelope front-loaded the entry with an ease-out cubic, reasoning that impact should feel abrupt. Simulating the exact per-frame state machine at the 60 fps cap showed that over a 0.09 s (5-frame) ramp it put a **0.17 scale jump in the first frame** and crawled through the rest — a cut wearing a curve's name, i.e. exactly the artefact the directive named. Fixed by making the asymmetry **duration-only** (~0.15 s in, 2.5–3.5× out) with **smootherstep** on both ramps (zero end-derivatives, so no kink where a ramp meets a flat stretch) and flooring every ease-in at ~9 frames. Re-verified: first-frame delta **0.010–0.016**, last-frame **≤0.0002**, phase boundaries continuous to **±0.0002**. Interpolation is logarithmic because the eye tracks the frame-to-frame *ratio* of the scale — measured, the log ramp's perceptual peak sits in the middle of the curve where smootherstep intends it, while linear drifts it to ~75% and kinks.
- **Rarity is enforced, not hoped for:** at most **2 non-K.O. shots per round, ≥7 s apart** (K.O. exempt), = **2.4–2.8 s of cinema in a 43–61 s round: 4–6% of its length, 7–9% of its clean hits**. Triggers are all deterministic — COUNTER, BREAKER, COMEBACK, tier-3 SUPER, K.O. — with **no RNG**, since a random crit would make identical reads look different and would be the first non-deterministic thing in a sim D-017 wants lockstep-able. **Damage untouched**: "critical" classifies the moment, not the numbers, because D-025's table is one session old.
- **Three real side-fixes the work exposed:** the killing blow had **no hit-stop at all** (`Resolve` returns before `ApplyImpactFx` on the death path — a KO landed softer than the jab before it); scaled `WaitForSeconds` in the round flow is **stretched by the cinematic it waits on** (the KO beat's 0.4 s would run ~2.7 s at 0.15×, its banner ~9 s), so the KO and TIME paths now hand time back first; and **online is locked out**, since dilating one peer's clock is a desync. **F3** toggles the system (`PlayerPrefs` `kr.slowmo`).
